# Impeccable

## Design Context

### Users
Michelle (and occasionally a companion at the table) — a private, passcode-locked
wine journal used almost entirely on a phone, often in low-key real-world moments:
at a restaurant table, in a wine shop, on the couch after opening a bottle. One
primary job: "log this bottle and whether I liked it, fast", and one secondary
job: "what was that wine I loved?" (search/browse) plus light learning via grape
profiles.

### Brand Personality
Cool, precise, curated. The app should feel like an exhibition catalogue for a
personal collection — the bottle photography is the art, the interface is the
gallery. Emotionally: calm confidence, quiet pride in the collection. Not cozy,
not scrapbook, not sommelier-formal.

### Aesthetic Direction
Modern gallery / exhibition catalogue. Photography-led layouts, generous white
space, cool precision in typography. A new typographic identity (previous
Fraunces + Schibsted Grotesk pairing is being retired). Light theme — used in
daylight at tables and shops, and photography reads best on a light ground.
Anti-references: generic AI dashboard aesthetics, wine-club kitsch (burgundy
gradients, script fonts, grape clip-art), cozy scrapbook.

### Design Principles
1. The photograph is the hero — the UI recedes; chrome earns its place or goes.
2. Gallery precision — exact alignment, a strict type scale, disciplined
   letterspaced caps used sparingly as wayfinding, never decoration.
3. Generous space over boxes — separation by whitespace and hairlines, not cards.
4. One accent, rarely — a single wine-derived accent reserved for "you liked it";
   everything else is ink on paper.
5. Phone-first, one-handed — primary actions at the thumb, type sized for arm's
   length at a dinner table.
