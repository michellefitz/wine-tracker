What the data supports

Everything a map needs, and the one thing missing

What a bottle stores today
ColumnTypeWhat's actually in it
countryfree text“Italy”, “Spain”, “Portugal”. Resolves reliably — countryCode() already maps 90-odd spellings and alternates to ISO codes.
regionfree text“Rioja Alta, Haro” · “Piedmont” · “Vinho Verde” · often empty. A subregion and a town crammed into one field, at whatever scale the label happened to print.
grapesjson arrayReliable, and a strong hint about region — Nebbiolo narrows Italy to a handful of places.
——No coordinates. No parent region. No controlled vocabulary.
Works today, no new data

A country-level map. Every bottle already resolves to an ISO code, so marks per country need nothing built.

Needs work

The region level — the part you actually want. “Piedmont” can't tell you Barolo from Alto Piemonte, and nothing knows one sits inside the other.

Three ways to close the gap
ApproachCostTrade
A gazetteer in the repo Free, instant, offline A table of wine regions with a parent and a rough centre, matched the way serving.ts already matches grapes. Needs curating — a few hundred entries covers most of what you'd drink. Nothing to migrate.
Ask at lookup time Free — same call The research lookup already runs per bottle and often knows the appellation the label field doesn't. Add a region path and a centre to its schema. Needs a column and a backfill.
Both, cached One call per new region Gazetteer first for the common cases, the model for anything unknown, kept in a regions table — the same shape the grape profiles already use.
The honest limitation

Some bottles will never place. A supermarket Prosecco whose label says only “Italy” has no region to find, and the map has to show that gracefully rather than guessing — a bottle in the country's margin, not a pin in the middle of Umbria. Roughly one in four of the labels you've photographed so far named no region at all.
